7.15.1. General Information & Architecture

The I²C interface (Inter Integrated Circuit) was developed by engineers at Philips as a straightforward way to connect integrated circuits and nodes within a single electronic system. The goal was to allow all integrated circuits to communicate using a shared, standardized interface to minimize interconnect complexity. The resulting two-wire serial bus is widely known as the I²C bus.

Key Characteristics of the I²C Bus:

  • Two Lines Required: SDA (Serial Data Line) for data transfer, and SCL (Serial Clock Line) for clock synchronization. Microcontroller pins connected to these lines are typically RC3/SCL and RC4/SDA.
  • Open-Drain Architecture: Drivers on the bus are open-drain type. Both SCL and SDA lines must be connected to high level ($V_{DD}$) via pull-up resistors. When the bus is idle, both lines stay HIGH.
  • Unique Slave Addresses: Every device connected to the bus possesses a unique hardware address used for target selection.
  • Master / Slave Roles: Communication is always initiated by a master device (typically a microcontroller), which controls clock generation ($SCL$) and frame sequence. The target device acts as a slave.
  • Multi-Master Capability: Multiple master devices can be connected to the same bus (multi-master mode).
  • Speed Modes: Standard Mode (100 kbit/s), Fast Mode (400 kbit/s), and High-Speed Mode (3.4 Mbit/s). Devices running at different supported speeds can coexist on the same physical bus.
  • Capacitive Limit: The total number of devices connected to the bus is constrained primarily by the total parasitic bus capacitance (maximum 400 pF).

7.15.1.2. Protocol Rules & Transfer Logic

Communication on the I²C bus strictly adheres to state conditions governed by $SCL$ and $SDA$:

1. Bus Idle & Start / Stop Conditions

  • Bus Idle: Both $SDA$ and $SCL$ remain HIGH.
  • START Condition ($S$): Initiated by the master by driving $SDA$ from HIGH to LOW while $SCL$ remains HIGH. This marks the bus as "busy".
  • Repeated START Condition ($Sr$): Issued during an ongoing transaction (without generating a STOP first) to switch transmission direction or register targeting cleanly.
  • STOP Condition ($P$): Initiated by the master by releasing $SDA$ from LOW to HIGH while $SCL$ is HIGH. This releases the bus back to the idle state.

2. Data Validity & Bit Sampling

Data on the $SDA$ line must remain stable throughout the entire HIGH period of the $SCL$ clock pulse. $SDA$ state transitions are only allowed while $SCL$ is LOW. Changing $SDA$ while $SCL$ is HIGH is exclusively interpreted as a START or STOP condition.

3. Address Frame & Acknowledgement (ACK / NACK)

Data is transmitted serially in 8-bit bytes (MSB first). After every 8 bits, a 9th clock pulse is generated by the master for acknowledgement:

  • ACK (Acknowledge): The receiver (Slave during write, Master during read) pulls $SDA$ LOW during the 9th clock pulse.
  • NACK (Not Acknowledge): The receiver leaves $SDA$ HIGH. Master uses NACK during the final read byte to signal to the slave that data transmission should cease before generating STOP.
